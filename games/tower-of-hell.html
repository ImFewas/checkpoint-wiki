Eternal Towers of Hell

Climb a randomly-assembled tower before the timer or the obstacles get you. No combat, no building — just movement and nerve.

Eternal Towers of Hell (formerly Juke's Towers of Hell and originally Kiddie's Towers of Hell) is a Roblox obby game by the ETERNITγ group, currently owned by aetrnalis. In it, you climb obbies (known as towers in this game) that range in difficulty from Easy to Unreal in order to progress through the game's many worlds.

The Ring Select is used to travel to these worlds (currently there is only two worlds, The Great Inferno and Spatial System) and easily access the Towers within. The Towers get progressively harder as you progress in these worlds, so it is recommended that you have some prior skill in obbies before attempting EToH.

Controls basics

Move

WASD or arrow keys on PC, joystick on mobile/console. Diagonal movement is usually a bit faster than pure cardinal directions.

Jump

Space bar. Jump distance and height are fixed, so most obstacles are solved by positioning before the jump, not mid-air correction.

Camera

Widen your camera distance if the default is too close — seeing the next two platforms ahead makes route-reading much easier.

Climbing tips general

  • Pause at branch points. Towers often split into multiple viable paths — a half-second to scan is cheaper than a wrong commit.
  • Don't chase the timer. Rushed jumps miss more often than deliberate ones; a clean route beats a fast, reckless one.
  • Learn to recognize obstacle types on sight (moving platforms, disappearing platforms, spinners, jump pads) so you react to the type, not each instance individually.
  • Edge-jump conservatively. Jumping from the very edge of a platform gives more distance but less margin for error — worth it on long gaps, not worth it on short ones.
If you keep falling at the same spot: it's usually a timing or angle issue, not a "the game is unfair" issue. Try approaching from a different angle or waiting one extra beat before jumping.

Reading the range: the decimal isn't just flavor — a 1.05 Easy tower and a 1.95 Easy tower can feel worlds apart even though they share a portal color. "Bottom," "Low," "Mid," "High," and "Peak" are the usual words you'll see for where in a tier something sits. Anything past Remorseless gets called Soul Crushing by the community; Horrific, Unreal, and Nil are non-canon — Nil towers in particular haven't even been given a rating yet.
The Too Hard rule: no tower rated above Catastrophic can ever be added to the actual game, and no Citadel can be rated Soul Crushing. This is why Horrific, Unreal, and Nil are marked non-canon above — they're tracked here for reference, but nothing in those three tiers will ever make it into the real game. The rule only applies to the actual game itself; fangames (CSCD, TEA, PoM, and others) aren't bound by it and can freely include towers and Citadels at those difficulties. Because Horrific, Unreal, and Nil towers will never occupy an official spot on the game's difficulty chart, their pages skip the "organized by position in the Difficulty Chart" breakdown that the canon tiers get.

Ratings shift over time as towers get revamped, deconfirmed, or rebalanced by the community — treat exact decimals as a snapshot rather than a fixed spec.
